## Understanding Household Fuel Impacts

Gasoline prices changed $0.42 in March 2022. Your household didn't just see a pump price rise—you felt it at the grocery store, in your commute time calculation, and in your monthly budget. The question isn't "What is crude oil today?" It's **"How much is this volatility actually costing me personally?"**

The FuelRipple Disruption Index translates the abstract concept of "price volatility" into concrete household cost impacts. This guide shows you how to track, predict, and budget for fuel cost disruptions using data that actually mirrors your economic reality.

## The Cost of Volatility, Not Just the Price

A common misconception: "Gas prices are at $3.50, so I budget $3.50/gallon."

But prices don't stay at $3.50. They move. And when they move, your household consumption patterns, commute times, and discretionary spending change.

**What researchers have found**:

- When gas prices jump 25¢+ in a month, household spending on fuel increases, but commute miles *don't* change proportionally. People keep commuting to work, school, and essential errands. Instead, they cut back on:
  - Discretionary road trips: visiting family, weekend travel (2–3% less in survey data)
  - Grocery shopping and errands consolidation (fewer trips, larger per-trip spending)
  - Dining and entertainment outside the home (measurable drop in rural areas)

- Households making $40,000–$60,000 per year are hit hardest. They spend 8–12% of income on fuel (vs. 2–3% for wealthy households). A $0.25/gallon increase means $40–50/month extra for a typical commuter.
- Low-income households often live farther from jobs, increasing their per-gallon annual burden. A 10% increase in gas prices increases total transportation cost by 5–8%, which crowds out budget for food, medicine, and rent.

**Enter the Disruption Index**: Instead of tracking raw price, it quantifies the *cost of volatility*—how much your household's fuel budget will swell when prices move.

## How FuelRipple's Disruption Index Works

The Disruption Index combines three metrics:

**1. Price Volatility (Annualized Standard Deviation)**

This measures how much prices are swinging week-to-week. 

- Stable market: prices move 1–2¢ per week → volatility is low
- Volatile market: prices move 5–10¢ per week → volatility is high

Formula: Calculate the standard deviation of the past 52 weeks of price change, then express as a percentage.

**2. Price Level Impact (Distance from Baseline)**

Even if volatility is moderate, prices being *high* matters more than prices being *low*.

- If prices are at $2.50 and swing $0.50, that's 20% volatility.
- If prices are at $4.00 and swing the same $0.50, that's 12.5% volatility, but the absolute cost impact is larger.

The Disruption Index weights high prices more heavily: a dollar of volatility at a $4.00 baseline matters more than a dollar at a $2.50 baseline.

**3. Persistence (Does it stick around?)**

A price spike that lasts 2 days is different from one that lasts 2 months.

- Spike that lasts 2 days: consumers absorb it once → moderate disruption
- Spike that lasts 8 weeks: your fuel budget grows every week for 2 months → high disruption

The Disruption Index measures *duration* of elevated prices using a 12-week rolling correlation between current prices and elevated-price periods.

## Beyond the Index: Travel Decisions

The Disruption Index doesn't just adjust your baseline; it should inform your *discretionary spending*.

**When Disruption Index < 30 (calm markets):**
- Gas prices are predictable; stable.
- Summer road trips and extra commuting are lower-cost than average.
- Consider bunching discretionary trips (visiting family, weekend travel) when likely to be cheapest.

**When Disruption Index 40–60 (moderate-to-elevated):**
- Prices may spike; high uncertainty.
- Consolidate errands; reduce discretionary trips.
- Fill up when prices dip; avoid running on "E."  
- Plan major road trips conservatively (assume higher fuel costs).

**When Disruption Index >70 (high volatility or crisis):**
- Prices unstable and likely elevated.
- Defer non-essential travel.
- Fill up when prices are locally soft.
- Consider carpool, public transit, or WFH for non-essential commutes.

## The Math Behind Smart Refueling

If you track the Disruption Index weekly, you can time refueling to capture local price dips:

- Average driver: 40 gallons/month.
- If Disruption Index rises from 35 → 65, prices typically rise 5–15¢ within 1–2 weeks.
- A strategic fill-up during the 35 window (before the spike) saves: 40 gal × $0.10 (midpoint) = $4.
- Over 12 months with 3–4 such cycles: $12–16/year in savings.

Small savings per event—but they add up, and they're essentially free if you're refueling anyway.
